FindingTwo separate languages exist. Sharp corners and hairline rules signal taste brand — a magazine you admire. Rounded corners, pills and medium-weight small text signal consumer app — a thing you use. Both are modern. Only one converts.
DecisionOn Mic is reached by tapping a link in an Instagram bio, on a phone, with the single job of taking a booking. That is app territory, not magazine territory. The v1 draft was built in the taste-brand language, which is why it read as dated to the intended audience.
DetailBody weight is 500–600, not 400, and base size is 12–13px, not 16px. Small, medium-weight text at high density is what a phone-native interface looks like. Large light text reads as a desktop website shrunk down.
01

Eight rules

Every decision below traces to one of these. If a future addition breaks one, the addition is wrong.

The thumb owns the bottom third

The primary action is never something you scroll to find. It lives in a sticky bar within thumb reach and stays there. Never a CTA that only exists at the bottom of the page.

One screen, one job

Every viewport has exactly one thing it wants you to do. Secondary actions are visually quieter by at least two steps — never two buttons of equal weight.

Mystery is the product

The venue stays hidden until 24h before. This is not a limitation to work around — it is the strongest conversion device on the page. Design it as a locked object that visibly wants to be opened.

Scarcity must be true

The seat meter shows real remaining seats from the database. Never fake urgency, fake countdowns, or "only 2 left" that resets. Gen Z identifies manufactured scarcity instantly and it costs trust permanently.

Motion confirms, never decorates

Every animation answers "did that work?" A press shrinks. A confirmation pops. A lock dissolves. Motion that exists only to look impressive gets cut.

Content is the interface

Reels are 9:16 and full-bleed, framed by nothing. No captions over images, no card chrome, no titles competing with the footage.

One accent, held

Tangerine marks what to tap and nothing else. Green and red are semantic only — state, never emphasis. Never a second brand colour for variety.

Speak like a person

"Book a seat", not "Register now". "You're in", not "Registration confirmed". Buttons say what happens next in the words a friend would use.

RuleBig type gets negative tracking. Small uppercase labels get positive tracking. Opposite directions — this single detail separates modern typesetting from amateur more reliably than any colour choice.
RuleEvery number on the site is Geist Mono with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. Dates, prices, seat counts, coupon numbers, countdowns. Numbers in the body face look like prose; numbers in mono look like data, and data feels trustworthy.
LoadThree families, six weights total. Subset to latin and self-host if the Kanpur mobile connection is a concern — Bricolage is variable, so it ships one file.

CutNo custom cursor, no scroll-jacking, no page transitions, no parallax. All four are desktop-only effects on a site where the overwhelming majority of traffic arrives from an Instagram bio link on a phone. They cost load time and deliver nothing to the actual user.
RequiredEvery animation is wrapped in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce). Content must be fully readable and bookable with JavaScript disabled — nothing is parked at opacity 0 waiting for an observer.
Hapticsnavigator.vibrate(12) on primary press, vibrate(40) on booking confirmed and drink claimed. Android only — iOS Safari ignores it silently, which is fine.

Build notes

Ordered by what breaks the experience most if skipped.

01

Sticky CTA bar with env(safe-area-inset-bottom) padding. Rises once, persists. This single element carries the conversion.

02

All inputs at 16px font-size minimum or iOS Safari zooms on focus and the page feels broken.

03

Seat meter reads real remaining capacity from the events table. Never a hardcoded or decorative number.

04

Venue address is never sent to the client before the unlock time — not hidden with CSS, not in the HTML, not in the JSON payload. Blur is a visual effect, not a security boundary.

05

Press state on every tappable element, including links and the Instagram tile. Inconsistency here is what makes a site feel like a document instead of an app.

06

Reels lazy-loaded, poster frame as a static image, video only on tap. Autoplaying six videos on Kanpur mobile data is the fastest way to lose the visitor.

07

Fonts subset to latin and self-hosted with font-display: swap. Bricolage is variable — one file covers every weight.

08

Test on a real mid-range Android on mobile data, not a desktop browser at 390px. That is the actual device.

CopyContent lands later. Every string in this spec is placeholder, but the shapes are not — headline is two to four words per line and never more than three lines, body blocks cap at two sentences, and button labels are two or three words. Copy that outgrows these shapes breaks the layout rather than the layout stretching to fit it.
PortfolioThe system extends to past jams, artist-of-the-month and a gallery without new tokens: reuse the reel rail for archive footage, the locked card for anything time-gated, and the pass for any credential. Adding a component should mean composing these, not inventing more.
